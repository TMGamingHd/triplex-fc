# Fault campaign: every fault, many inputs, safety properties on every frame

> What this is: the systematic test of how the flight-computer logic responds to every fault in `docs/FMEA.md`, over the
> whole range of each fault's inputs, with properties checked on every frame. All numbers in the tables below are
> generated by `python3 -m campaign.report` from the results of `python3 -m campaign.run`; nothing is typed in by hand.
> Everything runs through the real `core/` code (`tfc_replay`), deterministically, in software. Hardware effects
> (electrical, arbitration, real latency) are out of scope until the rig exists; section 8 lists what they will have to answer.

## 1. Why a campaign, and how real programs do it

Launch-vehicle and aircraft avionics are qualified by *fault injection*: for every failure mode in the FMEA (ECSS-Q-ST-30-02C,
SAE ARP4761, MIL-STD-1629A) a fault is injected into a software-in-the-loop or hardware-in-the-loop rig, and the response is
compared with the requirement. Two things distinguish a serious campaign from a demo: it covers the *range* of each fault
(not one magnitude at one instant), and it is judged by *properties that must always hold* rather than by a list of expected
outputs, so it can find the case nobody anticipated. This campaign does both, and then checks its own sharpness with mutation
testing (section 5).

## 2. What is covered

32 fault kinds (the first thirteen from the original peers, nineteen from the FMEA gap analysis), each over the parameter
values around its thresholds, on every node, from several start frames, in Triplex and in Duplex (two nodes), in a
moving and an at-rest vehicle, with several seeds for the random ones; plus recovery sequences with operator commands, two
simultaneous faults, cascades during probation, common-cause faults, boot-order and start-up edges, a last survivor with no
peers, a total loss, and ten-minute runs. A *scenario* is one such run through the manager.

The `ground_security` group sends forged, replayed, armed, expired and interlocked operator commands; `duplex_boundary` sweeps a Duplex bias around the 2x tolerance threshold over 20 seeds per magnitude. The `phase_*` groups start every fault kind at 40 different instants (the vehicle's motion is sinusoidal, so what a frozen or
stale value looks like depends on the moment). See ADR-016.

**Expectations.** From each fault's parameters and the documented thresholds (vote tolerances 1.0 dps / 0.02 g / 0.01 deg,
3-of-5 persistence, leaky count K=0.9 T=3, stuck limit 20, vote at 7 ms) each scenario is labelled **detect** (the faulty node
must end up isolated, within a latency bound), **ignore** (no node may be isolated and, in Triplex, no Safe request: sparse or
small trouble must not cost a healthy node), **gray** (within noise of a threshold: the outcome is *reported* but not judged,
because noise decides which side it falls on) or **any** (only the always-true properties apply). In Duplex a detect-class
fault may end in a Safe request instead of an isolation, because two nodes cannot always tell which one is wrong (ADR-008).

<!-- campaign:properties:end -->

M1-M8 hold in **every** scenario, whatever the faults: they are the manager's own consistency (the mode is Safe or follows the
healthy count; node states move only along the legal edges; a latched node never votes; a Safe request is sticky until cleared;
outputs are finite and bounded; a held output repeats the last good value exactly; a probation never starts while another runs,
except in total loss). S1-S4 hold when **one** node is faulty: no healthy node is ever isolated, the non-held output stays within
one vote tolerance of the truth (the motion the vehicle really made), no Safe request in Triplex, no readmission while the fault
is still active. The `E_*` codes are the advisory expectations above (missed detection, too slow, false isolation, wrong alarm,
wrong counter or final state); their absence means every labelled scenario came out as labelled.

## 5. Proving the campaign can fail: mutation testing

A campaign that cannot fail proves nothing. 72 deliberate bugs (`tools/mutation/mutations.py`) are injected, one at a time, into a
copy of the core: a hold that returns zero, a Safe flag that is not sticky, a latched node that still votes, a probation that cannot
fail, a strike limit that never disables, a tolerance doubled, an arbitration that compares with a standstill, a check removed from
the self-protection, and so on. Each mutant must be caught.

| Suite | Mutants | Caught | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| C++ tests (unit, property, fuzz, recovery, ground commands; ASan + UBSan) | 72 | 72 | `python3 tools/mutation/run_unit.py` |
| Fault campaign | 58 (the other 14 are C++-only, see below) | 58 | `python3 -m campaign.mutate` (stops at the first group that raises an anomaly) |

The first version of the campaign missed 4 of 12 mutants. That is how the always-true properties M7 (hold exactness) and M8 (one
probation at a time), the `commands_strikes` group and the readmission and counter checks came to exist. The C++ suite initially
missed 3 of 47, all exact-boundary cases (the bus-alarm threshold, the late-sequence allowance, the dwell after a failed
probation); tests for each were added and the mutants are now killed.

Building the frame-number check and the ground-command security added 23 mutants and found the same kind of gaps again: one survivor
in the C++ suite (a damaged frame fills its slot: no test sent a clean copy a cycle later) and five in the campaign (`duplicate` was only
a "gray" expectation so a tracker that accepted duplicates passed; no scenario sent a stale copy of a damaged frame; the exact edge of
the 250-frame ARM window; the Duplex decision band, now pinned by `duplex_boundary`, 20 seeds per magnitude; the exact edge of the
counter window). Each was closed with a test or a scenario, or recorded as unit-only with its reason.

Mutants the campaign cannot see by construction (the peers never present a bad configuration, flip a bit of the manager's memory,
overflow its command queue, send a NaN or a frame of the wrong length) are covered by the C++ tests alone and are excluded from the
campaign run; the fourteen are listed, with the reason for each, in `CAMPAIGN_SKIP` in `tools/mutation/mutations.py`.

## 6. Edge cases found, and what was done

"Fixed" means a regression test was written first, fails on the old code, and passes on the new. Severity is the worst consequence
for the vehicle in the scenario that triggers it.

| # | Trigger | What happened | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The **healthy node was isolated, the faulty node became the only voter and its frozen command became the actuator output**, with no Safe request. 63 of 80 start phases for `cmdstuck`; one wrong output frame before attribution for the others. | E12 | **Total loss**: every node latched (a 3-frame outage on all nodes, or the last healthy node failing after the others were out) | Probation compares a node with the voted output of the healthy nodes; there were none, so every probation frame was neutral for ever. ### Recommended next steps

The decisions taken on the open items, the work postponed until its trigger exists, and the design notes for each are in `docs/DEFERRED.md`. In short:

1. **Arrival-time monitoring** (E3, E11 option B): per-node arrival-margin telemetry and a non-latching warning first, a slot-window check once measured; needs the board.
2. **Safe action, self-test, analytical redundancy** (E6): define what the vehicle does in Safe when the actuator node exists; sensor self-test at power-up when the driver exists; analytical redundancy when the estimator exists.
3. **Membership agreement** between the flight computers (ADR-010 limit): today each decides alone.
4. **Real-uplink security** (E10): key provisioning, gateway authentication, constant-time comparison.
5. **Firmware requirements from ADR-018** for nodes B, C and ACT: take the frame number from SYNC, keep it through SYNC loss and sync-master takeover.
6. **Measure on the target**: WCET and stack of `end_frame` with the cycle counter, real CAN error rates (to re-tune the leaky count),
   inconsistent frame omission and duplication (a CAN property no software model reproduces).

Highlights, from the groups above:
- **Two simultaneous faults** (`pairs`, `new_pairs`): 1,557 combinations of two faults on two nodes with different start offsets.

  property violated.
- **Total loss** (`total_loss`): every case with at least two candidates recovers to Triplex; see E12/E20.
- **Ten-minute runs** (`long_run`, 60,000 frames each): healthy traffic, one corrupt frame in 50, 1% spikes, isolated late frames:

  within tens of frames by the leaky count.

## 8. What this does not show

- It judges *frame-level* behaviour on a synthetic motion profile (sinusoidal rates, gravity on z) and a synthetic estimator digest;
  the real estimator and controller arrive in M3.
- Time is virtual. Real latency, jitter, WCET and bus-arbitration effects need the rig (M2/M3).
- Electrical faults (open, short, bus-off, ground offsets), inconsistent frame omission and duplication on a real CAN bus,
  radiation and flash-ECC behaviour cannot be produced by software injection.
- Faults in all three replicas at once from a *shared software bug* (no design diversity) are invisible to any vote.
- "Gray" scenarios are reported, not judged; their outcomes are in the curves.

## 9. Reproducing

```bash
cmake -S . -B build/rel -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release && cmake --build build/rel --target tfc_replay
cd sim && export TFC_REPLAY_BIN=$PWD/../build/rel/tfc_replay
python3 -m campaign.run --strict --out /tmp/campaign.jsonl            # about 2 minutes on 12 cores; exit 1 on any anomaly
python3 -m campaign.report /tmp/campaign.jsonl --update-doc ../docs/FAULT_CAMPAIGN.md
python3 ../tools/mutation/run_unit.py                                 # 72 mutants against the C++ tests
python3 -m campaign.mutate                                            # the same against the campaign
python3 ../tools/coverage/core_coverage.py --list                     # structural coverage of core/
```
CI runs the campaign on every pull request (`--strict`) and the mutation checks weekly (`.github/workflows/`).
