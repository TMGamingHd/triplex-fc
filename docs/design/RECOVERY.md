# Bringing a stage back: the vehicle, the plant and the two ways down

> Status: **built on the host** (ADR-039, 10 Oct 2026). A vehicle with more than one stage gets a booster that flips, burns back, falls under grid fins, lights its engines for a landing and is **caught by a tower's arms**; a vehicle with one stage comes back **under parachutes**. Both fly end to end on the three flight computers of `docs/design/GNC.md` in the simulator (`tfc_mission`). The vehicle is a model built from public numbers and my estimates (section 2 lists each and says which); it is **not SpaceX's data and not a model of any flight article**, and its aerodynamics are engineering estimates, not wind-tunnel or CFD data. Nothing here has run on the hardware. Section 7 is what is not done.

## 1. What this adds to the simulator

`docs/design/GNC.md` is the flight computers' side: the navigation, the guidance and the sequencer. This document is the **plant** (the vehicle model the computers fly), the **vehicle** that was built to exercise it, and what was learned making a stage come back.

* **Engines the computers drive.** A stage marked `guided: true` runs the engines of the **groups** (0 to 7) the propulsion command's mask names, at the commanded throttle, never below the engine's lowest throttle (`min_throttle`) while it runs, with a limit on starts (`max_starts`) and an optional random failure to start (`start_failure_prob`, `start_seed`). Engines have a rise time and a decay time (`rise_s`, `tail_s`: first-order lags).
* **Control surfaces.** Flaps and grid fins (`surfaces` in the vehicle file), each on a hinge with a travel, a rate limit and a lag, **stowed until the "deploy surfaces" event**. Four command channels. `AERODYNAMICS.md` section 9 is the force model.
* **Parachutes.** A drogue and a main (`parachutes`), each released by an event, inflating with the square of the time over `inflation_s`, pulling along the velocity through the air at the attachment point; a canopy released above its `max_speed_ms` is torn off, and one is lost with its stage.
* **Separation.** The event `separate` lets the lowest stage go. The vehicle that stays is lighter and its centre of gravity moves to where the remaining parts' is: position and velocity shift by the offset along the axis and by the turning of the body, with a **separation impulse** (`separation_dv_ms`, the stage recoils by the same momentum) and a tip-off. The stage let go is recorded (`take_detached`) with everything needed to fly it on and becomes a **vehicle of its own** (`Vehicle6::spawn`, built from `stage_alone`).
* **Ground and tower.** With the landing model on, a vehicle that reaches the ground is judged on its speed, tilt and sideways speed (`crash_speed_ms`, `landing_tilt_deg`, `landing_lateral_ms`): `landed()` or `crashed()`. Its lowest point is the foot of its legs if they are out or the aft end. A **tower** (a scenario field) has arms at a height with an offset from the launch point; a stage whose **pins** (`catch_pin_x_m` from the stage's aft end) come down through the arms' height is caught if the pins are within the **capture radius (1.5 m)** of the arms' centre, sinking at no more than **2 m/s**, drifting at no more than **1.5 m/s** and tilted no more than **3 degrees**; otherwise it passes through and is judged by the ground.
* **Heating** (`Vehicle6::heating`): the stagnation-point flux on the nose by Sutton-Graves, `q = 1.7415e-4 sqrt(rho / R_n) V^3`, the flux over the belly as a factor of it, and the temperature the skin comes to when it radiates that flux away (`T = (q / (eps sigma))^(1/4)`). It is reported, not fed back (there is no thermal protection model).
* **The aerodynamics for the whole speed range** (`aero.full_regime`): see `AERODYNAMICS.md` sections 7 and 8.

## 2. The vehicle, its numbers and where each comes from

`vehicles/missions/starship_return.json`, generated by `tools/vehicle/gen_starship_return.py` (the numbers are in the script, with the same comments). **Public** numbers were read on 9 and 10 Oct 2026 from the SpaceX Starship page, Wikipedia (Super Heavy, Raptor, Starship flight 12) and a review of the V3 on newspaceeconomy.ca. **Mine** means an estimate nobody has published, or a number I tuned.

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Booster length, diameter | 72.3 m, 9 m | public |
| Booster engines | 33 Raptor 3; sea-level thrust 2.449 MN each (80.8 MN); 13 gimbal (the inner 3 and a ring of 10), 20 fixed | public (the gimbal split is the real vehicle's) |
| Raptor vacuum thrust, Isp | 2.584 MN (= sea level + 101,325 Pa x 1.33 m^2), 350 s | derived from the sea-level thrust and the exit area; Isp **mine** |

| Vacuum Raptor | 2.697 MN, Isp 380 s, exit area 4.5 m^2 | **mine** |
| Ship dry mass, payload | 100 t; 37.5 t | dry mass **mine** (Musk's 2021 figure); payload public (Flight 12's mass simulators) |
| Grid fins | **3**, at 0, 90 and 180 degrees, 11.1 m^2 each, +-35 degrees at 60 degrees a second | count public (Flight 12); size, travel and rate **mine** |
| Flight 12 timeline | max-Q 0:45, MECO 2:22, hot staging 2:24, boost-back 2:30, ship cut-off 8:11 (planned), apogee about 195 km | public |

The staging is at a stack mass chosen so that the booster keeps **650 t** of propellant (the mission's `booster burn` ends at 2,662,500 kg). A real booster keeps less; it is the heaviest landing the guidance has been made to handle, and it is the reason the landing burn uses 130 t. Lowering it to 300 t left the boost-back without enough to finish (the burn stopped at its reserve, 117 km short).

## 4. Smaller rockets: a single stage comes back under parachutes

`vehicles/missions/rocket_recovery.json`: 1,400 kg, one gimballed 60 kN engine for 24 s, four tail fins (it is stable by itself: the first version had none and was aerodynamically unstable enough that the gain design hit its limit and the loop rang), cold-gas thrusters for the attitude when the engine is off. Its mission is a pitch program, a coast to the apogee (22 km), and a `chute` phase: **a drogue at 6 km and a 250 m^2 main at 1.5 km** (events `chute0` and `chute1`), no attitude control. It lands in both modes (`tfc_mission ... --expect-landing`; the ideal mode forces the attitude only while there is a control phase). A vehicle with one stage has nothing to land with but the canopy: no boost-back, no landing burn, no catch. The touchdown is judged by the same ground model (here at up to 9 m/s and 40 degrees of tilt, set in the file).

## 5. How the plant is checked

`tests/test_landing_plant.cpp` (26 tests) and `tests/test_newtonian.cpp` (9): the plate model of a flap and a grid fin against hand calculations of the same formulas and the physics they must obey (a plate takes energy out of the flow, a stowed flap is nearly invisible, a plate broadside to the stream has the flat-plate drag, the lattice chokes through Mach 1, a fin stalls); the parachute's inflation and the **terminal speed its drag area gives** (a vehicle under it falls at `sqrt(2 m g / (rho CdA))`); the engines a stage is driven to run (the mask, the throttle, the lowest throttle, one start only, a start that fails); the surfaces' deployment and rate limit; the **separation conserving momentum and the centre of gravity of the whole**; the touchdown judged on speed and attitude; the legs; the **catch** (close and slow enough, and every way to miss); a vehicle that has not left the pad keeps the pad model; the full-speed-range aerodynamics agreeing with the build-up at low speed and following the Newtonian table at hypersonic speed, continuous through the blend, with the sign of the axial force changing flying tail first and a turning body damped. Newton's theory against its closed forms: a cone's drag is `Cp_max sin^2` of the half angle on the base, a hemisphere's half of that, a flat face's the stagnation coefficient, a cylinder across the flow four thirds of it per plan area.

## 6. What the tuning taught (so that the next vehicle does not repeat it)

In the order they were found, each of them a thing that made a closed-loop booster crash while the ideal-mode one landed:

1. **The ideal mode hides the attitude loop.** The first booster that caught in the ideal mode (attitude forced to the reference) crashed in the closed loop for nine reasons in a row; the ideal mode is the design's tool and a check of the guidance, never a result.
2. **Units.** The gains came out in degrees per radian and the loop took degrees: 57 times too high, the gimbal chattering at its limits.
3. **The accelerometer under thrust** pulled the estimated attitude 15 to 46 degrees off at hot staging.
4. **The roll command was computed and never applied** (voted, then stored in a member that nothing read); the booster rolled.
5. **Reference rate versus actuator authority**: a flip at 9 degrees a second on thrusters that give 0.026 rad/s^2.
6. **The engines' decay time** is an impulse: ignoring it left the booster 7 km past the site (and the ship 186 km high at apogee).
7. **The drag the predictor assumed** was not the plant's.
8. **The fin allocation** (one for the phase, then one per point; and scaled to the travel).
9. **Off-axis engines** make roll torque from a gimbal command; the symmetric three and thirteen do not.
10. **The attitude bandwidth** for a 40-degree tilt at ignition; the horizontal law's horizon; the engine count's effect on the gimbal's authority; the catch height by mass; the aim point under the arms.

## 7. What is not done

* The ship's **deorbit, entry and landing**. The plant has flaps and a belly-first Newtonian aerodynamics and the sequencer has a glide phase, but no mission flies the ship down.
* **Dispersions in the booster's return.** The mission flies the built-in mean wind profile (crossrange, `wind_scale` 1) with no gusts, no turbulence, no engine failure and every engine lighting when commanded; the Monte Carlo (`tfc_mc`) has not been pointed at it. One in nine GNSS noise seeds already misses the catch.
* **The firmware, the rig, the console and the viewer** do not fly or show a mission (`GNC.md` section 11): `tfc_mission --pose FILE --pose-body N` writes a pose file for one body (the booster is body 1) with the writer `tfc_fly --pose` uses, and the viewer's replay opens it without error; **I have not looked at it in a browser**, and the stack and the stage together, and the tower, are not drawn.
* **Real tower behaviour**: the arms are a capture window (a position, a sink speed, a drift and a tilt), not a mechanism.
* **A real vehicle's numbers**: see section 2.
